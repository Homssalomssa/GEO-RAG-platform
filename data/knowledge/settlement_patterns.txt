Illegal and Informal Settlements

Illegal settlements, also known as informal settlements, slums, or squatter settlements, are residential areas where inhabitants have no legal claim to the land. They are a major feature of urbanization in developing countries.

Physical Characteristics Detectable from Satellite Imagery:

1. Irregular Layout: Unlike planned developments, informal settlements lack a regular grid pattern. Buildings are placed without regard to setback regulations, lot boundaries, or fire safety distances.

2. Small Building Footprints: Structures in informal settlements typically have smaller footprints (20-60 square meters) compared to formal residential areas (80-200+ square meters).

3. High Building Density: Buildings are packed closely together with minimal spacing. Building density in informal settlements can exceed 50 structures per hectare, compared to 15-25 in planned residential areas.

4. Lack of Infrastructure: Informal settlements typically lack:
   - Paved roads (only narrow unpaved paths visible)
   - Visible utility infrastructure (power lines, water pipes)
   - Drainage systems
   - Public spaces or parks

5. Irregular Road Network: Instead of planned streets, informal settlements have narrow, winding paths that do not follow any planned pattern. These are typically unpaved and too narrow for vehicle access.

6. Proximity to Hazardous Areas: Informal settlements often develop in areas unsuitable for formal development, such as:
   - Flood plains and river banks
   - Steep hillsides
   - Adjacent to industrial zones
   - Along railway tracks

7. Roof Materials: In satellite imagery, informal structures often show heterogeneous roof materials (tin, plastic, thatch) visible as varied spectral signatures, unlike uniform roofing in planned developments.

Legal Framework:
- The UN defines informal settlements as areas where residents have no security of tenure
- Many countries have policies ranging from forced eviction to in-situ upgrading
- Regularization programs aim to provide legal tenure and basic services to existing settlements

Detection Methods:
- Object-based image analysis (OBIA) can identify informal settlements by texture, density, and pattern
- Machine learning classifiers can distinguish formal from informal built-up areas
- Multi-temporal analysis reveals rapid, unplanned growth patterns typical of informal settlement expansion

Settlement Upgrading Indicators:
- Introduction of paved roads within informal areas
- Regularization of building patterns
- Appearance of formal infrastructure (utility poles, drainage)
- Increased roof material uniformity (e.g., replacement of tin with tile)
